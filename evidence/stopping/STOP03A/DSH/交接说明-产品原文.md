## Target selection and named-target reasoning

- Named restore point: **`before_batch_cleanup`**, timeline 2, LSN **`0/C120000`**, event `RP-880`, created `2026-08-17T10:15:42Z`.
- The first destructive event is `TX-881` (`delete_active_customers_batch`) at LSN `0/C130000`.
- `TX-877` (`insert_invoice_INV-9912_amount_2190`) at `0/C100000` is before the target and is expected to survive; `TX-882` (`write_cleanup_audit_marker`) at `0/C140000` and `TX-881` are after the target and must be absent.
- A timestamp target is unsafe: `RP-880` (`0/C120000`) and `TX-881` (`0/C130000`) **share** the timestamp `2026-08-17T10:15:42Z`. Stopping at that timestamp is ambiguous and could stop after the destructive commit. The plan therefore uses `target_type: "name"` (the PostgreSQL `recovery_target_name` spelling) with `target_name: "before_batch_cleanup"` and `recovery_target_action: "pause"`.
- `target_lsn: "0/C120000"` records the resolved boundary; the authoritative selector is the name.

## WAL continuity

Recovery path on timeline 2, from backup end `0/A300000` to target `0/C120000`:

1. `00000002000000000000000A` — `0/A000000`–`0/AFFFFFF` (readable)
2. `00000002000000000000000B` — `0/B000000`–`0/BFFFFFF` (readable)
3. `00000002000000000000000C` — `0/C000000`–`0/CFFFFFF` (readable)

The chain is continuous and gap-free: segment A covers the backup end `0/A300000`, B follows at `0/B000000`, and C covers the target `0/C120000`. Segment D is not required because replay stops at `0/C120000`. Timeline-3 segments were not used; in particular `00000003000000000000000C` is unreadable and timeline 3 has no restore points. `restore_command_template` is the policy value `cp archive/%f %p`; no network fetch is involved.
